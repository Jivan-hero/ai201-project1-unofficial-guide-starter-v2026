House rules and table variants for Harbourmaster

None of these are official. They come from tables who wanted the game to be
slightly different and wrote down what happened. Each one says what it changes
and what it costs you, because most variants trade one thing for another.

Making the game shorter

Removing fifteen cards from the cargo deck before shuffling ends the game about
fifteen minutes earlier, which is the cleanest cut available. Two players is
also simply faster, at around thirty minutes, because removing a port from each
end of the coast track shortens everything downstream. What you lose in a
shortened game is the late game, which is where counting the cargo types that
have already appeared starts to pay off. If your table enjoys that part, cut
something else instead.

Making the game longer or tighter

Dealing every player one contract at the start rather than none adds pressure
from the first turn, and it favours whoever has played before, so it is a poor
choice for a mixed table. The Storm Season expansion adds about fifteen minutes
and is generally thought an improvement by people who have played the base game
more than three or four times. Below that, it is one more system to explain
during a game that is already the longest one that group has played.

Crew variants

The most common house rule is starting with four crew tokens instead of three.
It makes early turns more flexible and slightly cheapens the two points an
unspent token is worth at the end, since there is one more of them. A table that
finds the crew decision too painful will like it. A table that likes the
decision should leave it alone, because the tension between using a token now
and scoring it later is deliberate and is most of what the tokens are for.

Variants that do not work

Five players does not work, and the fix people reach for — dealing five market
cards instead of four — only makes the game affordable, not fast. The coast
track becomes congested, turns take too long, and the game runs close to two
hours. Raising the hold limit from three to four also does not work: cargo is
the constraint the whole game is built around, and removing it removes most of
the reason to plan a route at all.
