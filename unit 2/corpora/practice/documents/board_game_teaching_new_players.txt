Teaching Harbourmaster to a table that has never played it

A first game with new players runs closer to ninety minutes than the usual
fifty, and most of the difference is explanation. This is the order that keeps
that explanation short.

Start with the shape of the game

Say what winning looks like before you say what the pieces are. You sail along a
coast, buy cargo, sell it at ports that want it, and finish contracts. The game
ends when the cargo deck runs out and points come from coins, completed
contracts, and crew tokens you managed not to spend. Two minutes on that makes
every rule afterwards land somewhere.

Explain the market row before the first turn

The escalating cost of the market row is the rule new players forget most often,
and the worst moment to explain it is when someone has already reached for the
rightmost card. Say it early: leftmost is free, each slot right costs one more
coin, the rightmost costs three. Then say that the row refills as soon as a card
leaves it, because that is the second most forgotten rule and the two of them
compound.

What to leave out of the first explanation

Do not teach the expansion, the solo rules, or the alternative setups in a first
game. Do not read out pages six and seven of the rulebook either; they are
worked examples and they are more useful after a game than before one. Anything
you leave out can be added in the second game, and a table that is still
listening at the end of your explanation plays better than a table that heard
everything.

Questions you will be asked

Can I hold four cargo cards? No, three, and the limit applies at all times
rather than only at the end of a turn. Can I sell cargo at a port that does not
accept it? Yes, for one coin instead of two. Can I discard a contract I cannot
finish? Yes, at the start of your turn, for one coin. Does a crew token give me
another turn? No, one extra action inside the turn you spend it in.

During the game

Ask everyone to announce actions as they take them. Two actions per turn is easy
to miscount when people are talking, and a miscounted turn discovered three
turns later is not really fixable. Expect new players to sail more than they
load, and let them; noticing that themselves is worth more than being told.
