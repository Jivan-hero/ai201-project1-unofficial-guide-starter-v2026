A full walkthrough of a game of Harbourmaster

This is the whole game in one place, in the order it happens. The rulebook
covers the same ground across eight pages; this is the version to read if you
have already played once and want a reminder rather than a lesson.

Before the first turn

Lay out the coast board and give every player a boat board, three crew tokens,
and five coins. Shuffle the cargo deck and deal four cards face up to make the
market row. Decide which two cargo types the sixth port will accept, since that
port is the flexible one and it is chosen at setup rather than printed. Whoever
most recently ate fish goes first, and everyone else takes turns clockwise.

The market row and what it costs

The four face-up cargo cards are not priced equally. The leftmost card is free
to load. Each slot further right costs one more coin, so the second costs one,
the third costs two, and the rightmost costs three. When a card leaves the row,
slide the remaining cards left and deal a new one into the rightmost slot
immediately, not at the end of the turn. Doing the refill late is the single
most common mistake at a table of people who half-remember the rules, and it
quietly changes what everyone else can afford.

Actions on your turn

You take exactly two actions, chosen from sail, load, sell, and hire, and you
may take the same action twice. Sailing moves your boat one space along the
coast track. Loading takes one market card into your hold, which never holds
more than three cards. Selling puts a cargo card into a port: two coins if that
port accepts the type, one coin if it does not. A crew token may be spent to
take a third action, and the token goes back to the supply once spent.

Contracts

A contract card names cargo types and a destination port, and is claimed by
delivering those types to that port. You may have two open contracts at once and
no more. A contract you cannot finish can be discarded at the start of your turn
for one coin, which is usually worth doing sooner than people do it.

Ending the game and scoring

The game ends when the cargo deck runs out. Score one point per coin, three
points per completed contract, and two points for every crew token you never
spent. The player furthest along the coast track wins a tie.
